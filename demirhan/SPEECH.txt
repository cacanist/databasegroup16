I’m going to walk you through the User Interface capabilities. We took your specific requests—including the new expanded scope for tracking page suggestions—and mapped them to our new 3NF architecture to show you exactly what this system can do."



[0:30 - 2:00] Analytics & Reporting (Questions 1 & 2)

"Let’s start with visibility. You asked for the ability to list all visitors and the browsers they use. In our old unnormalized system, tracking this was messy. Now, we use a clean query that joins our new VISITOR, SESSION, and BROWSER_INFO tables. Even if a user has registered but hasn’t logged a session yet, our system ensures they still appear on your lists. No ghost users, no missing data.
You also wanted to track seasonality—specifically, the number of visitors seen in each month. Because we split out temporal data into a TIME_CONTEXT table, we can easily group your traffic by month. More importantly, we count distinct visitor IDs. This means if one highly active user visits 50 times in November, they only count as one unique visitor. You get mathematically accurate unique traffic metrics, completely free from inflation."



[2:00 - 3:30] Operations & Tracking (Questions 3 & 4)

"Next, let’s look at daily operations. You asked how to change the region for a visitor. In the old spreadsheet model, you’d have to update every single row that visitor ever generated. Today, Region is smartly linked to Country via a lookup table. If a user moves from Germany to Japan, we update their country exactly once in the VISITOR table, and the system automatically resolves their new region across the entire database.
We also integrated your new request: tracking specific page visits and user suggestions. When we add a new page visit to the system now, it doesn't just say 'they looked at products.' It records the exact URL, the time spent, the scroll depth, and—crucially—whether that visit was organic, or if it was driven by a specific Suggestion_ID. You now have pixel-perfect attribution to calculate the exact ROI of your user-suggestion feature."



[3:30 - 4:15] Compliance & Security (Question 5)

"Finally, data privacy. When you need to remove a visitor from the database, you cannot afford to leave fragmented data behind, nor can you afford to accidentally delete the pages they visited.
We built a 7-step cascading delete protocol. It safely unwires the user from the database bottom-up. It nullifies any suggestion credits they gave to other users, deletes their analytics, clears their sessions, and finally removes their profile. It is a surgical removal that keeps your referential integrity 100% intact."
